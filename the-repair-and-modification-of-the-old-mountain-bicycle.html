The repair and modification of the old mountain bicycle

Date Mon 03 March 2025

Replacement bearings for my old bike worked well

I have "inherited" an old mountain bike from my friend to bike to class. It is a pretty standard design, with a nice Aluminium frame, V-brakes for both wheels, with a 21-speed transmission. During new-year, I decided to renovate the bike to match the new year theme, so without understanding the internal construction, I unscrewed the front wheel, and unscrewed the fasteners that held what seems to be a "nut". Soon, a plethora of dirty grease came out, among with 20 metal balls that I can not find anymore.

In the 21st century, apparently I did not expect a loose ball bearing to be existent. Bikes however, seem to adapt this design, for not only the wheels, but also the bottom bracket, and the clutch mechanism for the flywheel. Obviously a replacement was needed - I cannot ride the bike with no balls in the bearing. Instead of looking for 5mm bearing balls, I found an interesting alternative on Taobao - a conversion shaft that allows the usage of standard bearing piece. It is simply a steel rod, with threads on both ends, and a larger diameter in the middle to keep the bearing in place. Such kits were extremely low cost - although the quality seems decent. It would not be a too difficult piece to machine myself, but since I don't have a lathe around - maybe not.

Upon receiving the kit (which took a while due to international shipping), it seemed excellent. I have separate '6900' bearings, which are 10mm inner diameter, 22mm outer diameter, to match the front axle. These LYC branded bearings seems very legit, they work just fine.

For the bottom bracket there's a more problematic situation - I could not remove the mounting with the tools I have. So instead of replacement bearings, I had to only replace the bearing balls inside. The wear is pretty obvious, not only by the numerical measurements, but also the peddle shakes a bit when being driven.

There is more repair going on the way, for example the braking cable assembly for the back. Still, overall this was a fun experience, and I have collected enough spare parts/tools to open a bike shop.
